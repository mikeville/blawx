feature: the dorsal fin — a tall sickle-shaped blade rising off the back at the shoulder, oversized to reach roughly double the torso's own height (torso tops out around y7-8, the fin tip climbs to y12). It is also carved thin (only 3 cells deep) against the 5-cell-deep torso beneath it, so it reads as a blade standing proud of the body rather than a lump fused into it. This is the one shape that says "dolphin" and not "fish" or "shark" or "seal."
pose: level glide, snout pointed and tapering to a single point at the leading (right) edge, tail fluke flared wide at the trailing (left) edge and lifted slightly off the belly line. At the 30° iso camera the fin's thin blade will visibly separate from the torso's bulk (different depth reads as a visible ridge/step in top and right-side light), and the flared, deep tail footprint versus the narrow, shallow snout footprint gives the top view a clear front-to-back taper instead of a uniform slab.
depth plan: real fusiform proportions — long torso (~40% of length) between a flared tail and a tapering beaked snout, thin dorsal fin at the shoulder. Per-region extents: tail fluke (x0-1) y3-8 h3-6, z1-4 shallow/flared; tail stock (x2-3) y0-5 h5-6, z1-4 still carrying the flare; torso (x4-6 and x9-10) y0-7/8 h7-8, z0-4 full 5-cell bulk; dorsal fin saddle (x7-8) y0-12 h13 but pinched to z1-3, only 3 cells deep versus the torso's 5; head/rostrum (x11-15) y tapers 0-5 down to a single row at the tip, z narrows from 3 to 1 cell — thin beaked snout.
