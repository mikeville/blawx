feature: the long curling tail, a monkey's single most distinctive silhouette element at this scale. I run it almost the full height of the sculpture (rows 0-9 of 16) as a thin arm that hooks into a tight spiral above the shoulders, kept clearly separated from the round head so the two never merge into one blob.
pose: the monkey sits upright in side profile, facing right, body compact and grounded low, so the tail's hook reads as a distinct silhouette poking up and back from the shoulders even from the 30-degree isometric camera (front+top+right). Sitting keeps the mass low while the tail supplies height and drama above it, and a thin near-depth leg (shallow front-to-back) keeps the seated stance from reading as one constant-thickness slab.
depth plan: real proportions of a compact seated primate: round head about 2/3 the torso's depth; bulky torso the deepest part at full 6-cell depth; a bent leg that narrows to a thin near-side limb at shin height (z0-1 only, well short of the torso's full depth); flat feet spanning the full depth where they meet the ground; a thin 2-cell-deep tail that only occupies the mid-depth band (z2-3) as it hooks up past the head. Per-part spans: head z1-4 y-rows0-6; tail z2-3 y-rows0-9; torso z0-5 y-rows6-11; leg z0-1 y-rows12-13; feet z0-5 y-rows14-15.
