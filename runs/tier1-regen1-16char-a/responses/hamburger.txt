feature: the domed top bun — a rounded, swelling cap that rises above the flat layer stack beneath it. I oversize it to 6 of the 16 rows (more than a third of the total height) and taper it through three widening steps (6→8→10 cells wide) before it settles into the bun's straight sides, so the dome reads as a distinct rounded mass sitting on the stack rather than a flat-topped box.
pose: classic side-on burger stack, viewed straight on — dome bun on top, then a lettuce layer poking out past the bun's edges, two patty rows, a cheese layer also poking out past its neighbors, and a flatter bottom bun tapering to a small rounded footprint on the ground. Because lettuce and cheese are each a cell wider (and deeper) than the bun/patty rows directly above and below them, the stack reads as distinct overlapping layers at the 30° iso angle, not a single tapered cone — the top view's oval bulge and the side view's alternating depth bands both carry that same overhang cue.
depth plan: real burger proportions are a short, wide cylindrical stack, wider than it is deep. Per region, front width (x) / depth (z) / height rows (y, printed top-to-bottom): dome bun, rows 1-6, width tapers 6→8→10→12→12→12, depth tapers 2→3→4→4→4→4 (the dome is both the narrowest AND shallowest region, so it reads as a small rounded cap). Lettuce, row 7, width 14, full depth 6 (pokes out past the bun in both x and z). Patty, rows 8-9, width 12, depth 4 (pulled back in, like meat sitting inside the bun). Cheese, row 10, width 14, full depth 6 (pokes out again, echoing the lettuce). Bottom bun, rows 11-16, width 14→14→12→12→10→8, depth 6→6→4→4→3→3, tapering to a small rounded base that still touches the ground at row 16.
