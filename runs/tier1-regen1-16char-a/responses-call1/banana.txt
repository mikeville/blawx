feature: the curve — a banana's arc is its whole identity, so I exaggerate it into a dramatic hook rather than a straight tube: a short blunt stem hugs low near the ground at the left, the body sweeps down to rest its thick belly flat on the floor across the middle, and the far end curls up and back to a sharp, elevated point near the top-right — much higher than the stem, so the two ends are visibly asymmetric and the arc reads unmistakably.
pose: lying on its convex belly, both ends lifting off the ground at different heights (short stem barely raised, long tip curled way up) — this reads as a strong diagonal/hooked arc at the 30 degree iso camera because the silhouette's height changes continuously across x, and because the front-to-back depth is also thickest at the grounded belly and thinnest at both ends, rounding the tube in 3D instead of reading as a flat extruded cutout.
depth plan: real banana is a slender curved cylinder, round in cross-section, thickest at the belly and tapering to a blunt stem and a pointed tip. Per-region: stem (x:0-1) short and thin, z:1-3, y:4-8; belly (x:4-8, thickest and lowest part, resting on the ground) full depth z:0-4, y:0-7; shoulder toward the tip (x:9-11) z:1-4, y:2-9; curling tip (x:12-15) thins from z:2-3 down to a single z:2 cell at the very point, y:6-11. Depth shrinks smoothly from 5 cells at the belly to 1 cell at the tip, matching the front-view thickness taper so the sculpture reads as a rounded tube, not a flat slab.
