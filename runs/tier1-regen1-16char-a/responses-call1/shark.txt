feature: the tall triangular dorsal fin rising off the mid-back, paired with the tall upper lobe of the tail fin — two raised fin peaks against an otherwise low, sleek body is the one silhouette that reads "shark" instead of generic fish. Both are exaggerated well past real proportions (the dorsal fin alone spans a third of the body's height) so they survive at 16x16.
pose: side profile (the canonical swimming silhouette) — pointed snout at one end, torpedo body, asymmetric heterocercal tail at the other. The dorsal fin and tail's upper lobe both rise clear above the body's normal roofline, so at the 30° iso camera they read as two distinct raised blades rather than being absorbed into the back's top edge.
depth plan: long tapered torpedo body, roughly 4x longer than tall, laterally thin (real sharks are flattened side-to-side). Head/torso (x2-10) is the thickest region, z:1-4 (4 cells deep). The snout tip (x0-1) and the peduncle/tail (x11-15) taper to a thin 2-cell depth, z:2-3, matching the whip-like tail stock and pointed nose. The dorsal fin (top of x5-9) and pectoral fin (bottom of x4-7) are thin blade-like plates, z:2-3, nested inside the body's depth rather than extruding the torso's full thickness — so they read as fins, not slab continuations of the body.
