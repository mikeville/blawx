feature: the long curling tail, a monkey's single most distinctive silhouette element at this scale. I run it almost the full height of the sculpture (rows 0-9 of 16) as a thin arm that hooks into a tight spiral above the shoulders, kept clearly separated from the round head so the two never merge into one blob.
pose: the monkey sits upright in side profile, facing right, body compact and grounded low, so the tail's hook reads as a distinct silhouette poking up and back from the shoulders even from the 30-degree isometric camera (front+top+right). Sitting keeps the mass low while the tail supplies height and drama above it, and the two bent legs at different depths give the seated stance a real 3D stance rather than a flat cutout.
depth plan: real proportions of a compact seated primate: round head about 1/3 the torso's depth; bulky torso the deepest part at full 6-cell depth; two bent legs sitting at different front-to-back depths (near leg z0-1, far leg z4-5, gap between at shin height); flat feet spanning the full depth where they meet the ground; a thin 2-cell-deep tail that only occupies the mid-depth band (z2-3) as it hooks up past the head. Per-part spans: head z1-4 y-rows0-6; tail z2-3 y-rows0-9; torso z0-5 y-rows6-11; legs z0-1/z4-5 y-rows12-13; feet z0-5 y-rows14-15.
