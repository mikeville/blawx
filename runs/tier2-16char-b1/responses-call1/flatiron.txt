feature: the tall arched handle rising off the back of the wedge-shaped body — a solid raised bump/ridge (never an open loop, since the mask must stay solid) that domes up to nearly triple the body's own low height. That single vertical spike above an otherwise low, flat wedge is what reads as "iron" rather than a generic doorstop.

pose: shown in side profile with the pointed tip at the left (x=0) and the wide, tall heel/handle end at the right (x=15) — the standard flat-icon orientation for a clothes iron. At the 30° isometric camera this profile plus the off-center handle bump reads as a solid tapering wedge with a grip on top, not a flat cutout, and the footprint (visible on the top facet) narrows to a point at the tip and widens toward the heel, confirming the taper in 3D.

depth plan: real proportions — a low, long wedge body about 16 units long, pointed at the front and squared at the back, topped by a much taller, narrower handle rising off the back third. Per-region z(depth)/y(height): tip x:0-1 z:2-3 (thin, y:0 only — a one-cell point); front slope x:2-5 z:2-3 (thin, y:0-1); mid wedge x:6-8 z:1-4 (widening, y:0-3); body/heel x:9-15 z:0-5 (full depth, y:0-5 — the widest, lowest mass, the sole plate); handle riser x:10-13 z:2-3 (thin again, y:6-10, centered in the body's depth); handle peak x:11-12 z:2-3 (y:11-13, narrowing further at the rounded top).
