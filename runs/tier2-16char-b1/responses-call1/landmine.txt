feature: the horn cluster — five thick conical spikes (one long vertical top horn, two horizontal side horns stretched to the full 16-cell width, two diagonal upper-corner horns) bristling off a round central ball. Horns are oversized to reach the very top row and both left/right edges of the grid, so even at 16x16 they read as jagged spikes, not bumps on a plain circle.
pose: the ball sits centered with horns radiating outward in a star pattern (straight up, upper-left, upper-right, straight left, straight right) instead of one horn pointing straight at the viewer. At the 30° iso camera this means the top horn's peak, the full horizontal thrust of the left/right horns, and the ball's own curvature are all visible at once — a horn aimed dead at the camera would foreshorten into a dot, but these radiating horns each keep a visible length from the iso angle.
depth plan: real naval-mine bodies are near-spherical, about as deep as they are tall, with thin conical horns roughly a third of the body's diameter. Ball core (front rows 3-15, cols 3-12): deepest at the equator (rows 6-9, z0-5, full 6 cells) tapering to z1-4 at rows 4 and 10-12, and z1-3 at rows 3 and 13 — this is the sphere rounding off in depth, not a constant-thickness slab. Top horn (rows 0-2, cols 6-9): thin, z2-3 only. Left/right horns (tip at row 8 cols 0-2/13-15, tapering at rows 7 and 9): thin, riding the shallow z2-3 band, projecting sideways rather than front-to-back. Diagonal corner horns (rows 3-5, cols 1-2/13-14): also thin, sharing the ball's shallow z1-3 band at those rows. Base pad (rows 14-15, cols 6-9): a flattened ground-contact patch, z2-3, distinctly thinner than the fat equator above it.
