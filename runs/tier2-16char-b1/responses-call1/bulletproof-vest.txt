feature: the two shoulder straps that arc up and over the top of the torso plate, framing a deep V-neck notch between them — exaggerated by height (they stand a full 4 rows above the torso) and by depth (they run the whole 6-cell front-to-back span, while the torso plate is only 2 cells thick), so in iso they read as loops going over the shoulder to the back — the one cue that says "vest" instead of "shield" or "slab."
pose: the vest is posed flat-front, worn-and-viewed-head-on with the chest panel facing the camera, the canonical flat-icon orientation for body armor. At the 30° iso camera the thin torso plate reads as a flat panel while the straps, 6 cells deep against the torso's 2, visibly project backward and over the top, giving the silhouette real volume instead of a flat extruded cutout.
depth plan: torso panel (chest to hip, y0-11) is a wide, mostly-rectangular plate with a slight waist taper — x1-14 at chest and hips narrowing to x2-13 at the waist — held to z0-1 (2 cells thick), like a thin armor plate. The two shoulder straps (y12-15, x2-4 and x11-13) run the full z0-5 (6 cells) front-to-back, arcing from the chest, over the shoulder, to the back — the one part of the sculpture that isn't shallow.
