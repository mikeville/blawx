feature: the tall, backward-curling upturned prow (the ferro di prua) at the bow — I stretch it to 14 cells tall against a 3-cell-tall hull, over 4x the hull's height, so the hook silhouette reads "gondola" before the boat body is even parsed.
pose: shown in full broadside profile (the boat's length spans the 16-wide front view) with the flat hull grounded on the bottom row and the hook prow rising off one end; at the 30-degree iso camera the tall thin blade sits proud of the low flat deck like a spike breaking the roofline, while the narrow beam visible in the top/side views confirms a slender single-oar craft, not a barge.
depth plan: hull deck is 3 cells tall (y0-2) at full beam (z0-3) across the mid-length (x4-11); the bow and stern tips taper to a narrower half-beam (z1-2) at that same low deck height; the bow's blade then rises off its tip from y3 up to y13, narrowing from 3 cells wide (x1-3) to 2 cells (x1-2) before hooking back out to x2-3 at the very top; the stern gets a much shorter answering upturn, only to y6-7, so the two ends read as bow and stern rather than mirror twins.
