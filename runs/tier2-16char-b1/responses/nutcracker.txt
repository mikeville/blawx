feature: the tall cylindrical soldier's hat with a flared fur brim at its base — I oversize it to nearly a third of the whole figure's height (5 of 16 rows) and keep it narrower than the head below, so a slim tower sits on a wider face. That silhouette — thin tall hat, flared collar, wide blocky body beneath — reads as a nutcracker toy soldier rather than a generic figure.
pose: figure stands rigid at attention, arms fused into a wide block torso, on a flared wooden base — a stiff frontal military stance, symmetric left-right. At the 30° isometric camera the face is the frontmost surface (shallow, z:0-2) while the hat sits recessed just behind it (z:1-2) and the torso and base are the deepest, back-set mass (z:2-4), so the camera sees the footprint genuinely narrow at the face and swell toward the torso/base behind it — a rounded, tapered profile, not a flat stacked slab.
depth plan: real nutcracker = tall narrow figure (~1:2.5 width:height) topped by a disproportionately tall cylindrical hat with a flared brim, over a face, over a torso wider than the head, standing on a flared base. Per region: hat cylinder z:1-2 (depth 2), y:12-15 (4 tall), x:6-9 — thin, rising directly off the brim. Hat brim z:1-2 (depth 2), y:11 (1 tall), x:4-11 — flares past the cylinder in width only. Head z:0-2 (depth 3), y:8-10 (3 tall), x:5-10 — face is the shallowest, frontmost region. Body z:2-4 (depth 3), y:2-7 (6 tall), x:3-12 — the deepest, back-set mass (chest+arms), wider than the head. Base z:3-4 (depth 2), y:0-1 (2 tall), x:2-13 — the widest region, set at the very back of the depth range under the body.
bounds x:2-13 y:0-15 z:0-4
