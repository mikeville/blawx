feature: the crowned roof cap — a projecting cornice sits atop the shaft, x:4-11 (8 wide) against a shaft of x:5-10 (6 wide), echoed by a matching flared plinth base at the floor, x:3-12 (10 wide). The cap-over-shaft step is exaggerated to roughly 1.3x the shaft's width so it survives at 16px.
pose: the booth stands frontal and symmetric — a phone booth has no privileged "side," so the front face is drawn straight-on and centered. At the 30-degree iso camera the cornice's overhang and the plinth's flare both catch top light and throw a visible side-lit step, breaking the shaft into cap / body / base the way a real booth's cornice and base course do.
depth plan: real booth proportions — a slender vertical cabinet (~1:2.7 width:height) topped by a shallow overhanging cornice and set on a shallow flared plinth. To keep the object from reading as one continuous vertical slab in profile, depth is staggered by height tier instead of stacked at constant z: plinth (rows0-2 from floor, y0-2) sits at the near/front edge z0-1; the shaft's lower half (5 rows) sits at z1-3, bridging the plinth by one lane; the shaft's upper half (5 rows) sits at z3-4, bridging the lower half by one lane; the cap (top 3 rows) sits at the back edge z4-5, bridging the upper shaft by one lane. Each tier only shares a single z-lane with its neighbor, so no column runs the object's full height — front-to-back the booth steps forward as it descends, like a cornice that oversails a recessed body over a projecting base course. Max depth used is 6 cells (z0-5) but no single (x,z) column spans more than 10 of the 16 rows.
